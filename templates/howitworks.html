The System Manual.

From the moment you boot the local server to the second a threat is neutralized, here is exactly how the NetSentinel architecture protects your environment.

Phase 1

Network Initialization & Discovery

When you launch NetSentinel, the system immediately binds to your local network interface. It uses advanced ARP and Nmap protocols to sweep your environment, identifying every connected laptop, phone, IoT device, and their exact MAC Vendor signatures.

192.168.1.5 Apple, Inc.
192.168.1.12 Samsung Elec.
192.168.1.104 Unknown Vendor
Phase 2

Live Packet Sniffing

Once the topology is mapped, the background thread begins silently capturing traffic. Using Python's Scapy library, it extracts crucial metadata from every packet—tracking source IPs, destination ports, and total byte loads without reading your private payloads.

> sniffing traffic on eth0...
[UDP] 192.168.1.5:443 -> 8.8.8.8
[TCP] 192.168.1.12:80 -> 104.21.x.x
[TCP] 192.168.1.104:4444 -> 45.33.x.x
[TCP] 192.168.1.104:4444 -> 45.33.x.x
Phase 3

AI Threat Evaluation

Raw data isn't enough. NetSentinel aggregates the traffic statistics and feeds them into our Machine Learning detector. The AI cross-references packet frequency and port behaviors against known attack vectors like DDoS, Port Scans, and Reverse Shells.

Anomaly Score 94% RISK

Signature Match: High-frequency outbound traffic on non-standard port (4444).

Phase 4

Active Firewall Neutralization

When a critical threat is identified, you don't need to open your terminal. Clicking "Block IP" in the NetSentinel dashboard triggers a direct system-level command, instantly writing a hard-block rule to your Windows Defender Firewall.

Connection Severed

netsh advfirewall firewall add rule name="Block 192.168.1.104" dir=in action=block
